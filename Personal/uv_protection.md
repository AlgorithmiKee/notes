# UV Protection

## Abstract

This article explains the necessity of ultraviolet (UV) protection and gives tips on choosing the right sunscreen. UV radiation from the sun can cause skin damage, premature aging, and increase the risk of skin cancer. Therefore, it is crucial to protect our skin from UV rays.

## UV Light

UV light is a type of electromagnetic wave emitted by the sun.

UV light reaching the Earth's surface is divided into three categories by wavelength: UVA, UVB, and UVC.

* UVA (320-400 nm): UVA rays penetrate deep into the skin and can cause premature aging and wrinkles. Accumulated exposure to UVA rays increases the risk of skin cancer.
* UVB (290-320 nm): UVB rays have a shorter wavelength and less penetration than UVA rays, but they are the primary cause of sunburn and the main driver of skin cancer.
* UVC (100-290 nm): UVC rays have the shortest wavelength and most damaging potential, but they are mostly absorbed by the Earth's atmosphere and negligible at the surface.

As the wavelength decreases, the energy of the photons increases while the penetration depth decreases.

### Interaction of UV Rays with Matters

**Interaction of UV rays with atmosphere:**
UV rays interact with the Earth's atmosphere, where they can be absorbed or scattered by molecules and particles. The ozone layer particularly absorbs most (nearly 100%) of the harmful UVC and a significant portion (about 95%) of UVB rays. However, the atmosphere filters only about 5% of UVA rays, allowing them to reach the Earth's surface.

The intensity of UVB is highly dependent on the angle of the sun. In midday, UVB rays travels a shorter path through the atmosphere and are thus more intense compared to early morning or late afternoon. In contrast, UVA rays are less affected by the angle of the sun and maintain a more consistent intensity throughout the day.

**Interaction of UV rays with skin:**
The short-wavelength UVB rays reaches only the epidermis. It helps the synthesis of vitamin D but also contributes to sunburn. UVB rays are particularly effective at causing DNA damage, which increases the risk of skin cancer. Although UVA rays is less energetic, they can penetrate deeper into the dermis and damage collagen and elastin fibers, leading to premature aging and wrinkles.

**Interaction of UV rays with reflective surfaces:**
Both UVA and UVB rays follow the law of reflection. However, UVB rays are more pronounced in diffuse reflection, while UVA rays are more likely to be reflected specularly. Surfaces appearing smooth for UVA rays may appear rough for UVB rays. Diffuse reflection of UVB rays can increase the overall UV exposure, especially in environments with reflective surfaces such as snow, sand, or water.

**Interaction of UV rays with glass:**
Most common glass types, such as window glass, can block UVB rays but allow UVA rays to pass through. Intuitively, standard window glass appears opaque to UVB rays, but it is transparent to UVA rays and visible light. Therefore, UVA protection is necessary even indoors or in cars.
